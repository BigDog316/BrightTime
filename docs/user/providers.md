# BrightTime for organisations

This guide is for the people who run activities: an organisation's owners, admins and staff. Most
of it happens in the **console** on the website. Taking attendance on a phone happens in the app.

## Joining BrightTime

1. Open **List your organisation** (`/providers/apply`) and fill in three short sections:
   **You**, **Your organisation**, and **Where you run activities** (your main site; add more
   later).
2. We email you. If the address is new to BrightTime, the email has a link to choose a password.
   If you were signed in when you applied, you're the organisation's **owner**.
3. A **network organiser** reviews the application, usually within a few working days. They
   check your contact details, your website, your safeguarding policy and, where needed, your
   insurance. They may ask for changes: the console then shows what they asked for, and **Send
   back for review** on your **Profile** returns it to them once you've made the changes.
4. **Meanwhile you can draft listings.** They're published once you're approved.

If someone invites you to join an organisation, the invitation appears under **Invitations** when
you sign in. **Accept** or **Decline** it there.

Sign in, then open **Your organisations** to reach the console. Each organisation has its own set
of tabs: **Listings, Profile, Sites, Staff, Import, Forms, Applications, Attendance, Fees and
payments, Messages** and **Reports**. You see only the tabs your role allows.

## Who can do what

Each person has one role in each organisation.

| | Owner, admin | Staff |
|---|:-:|:-:|
| Draft, edit, submit, preview and copy listings | ● | ● |
| Add and edit sites | ● | ● |
| See and decide applications, offer places from the waitlist | ● | ● |
| Take attendance and correct it | ● | ● |
| Message families, send announcements | ● | ● |
| Download reports | ● | ● |
| Edit the organisation's profile | ● | |
| Invite staff, change roles, remove people | ● | |
| Import listings | ● | |
| Write forms, waivers and eligibility rules | ● | |
| Set an activity's places | ● | |
| Set fees, waive a fee, mark a payment received | ● | |

An organisation can also give someone the **mentor** or **viewer** role. Neither can use the
applications, attendance, messages, payments or reports pages.

## Your organisation: profile, sites and staff

**Profile.** Your **name** (the one families know), **type of organisation**, **what you do** (a
few sentences for your public page), **website**, **public email** and **phone**, and the
**languages your staff speak**. Your **legal name** is seen only by network organisers. If two
people edit the profile at once, the second save is refused rather than overwriting the first:
reload and make your changes again.

**Sites** are the places where you run activities. For each site:
- its **name** (imports match sites by name), **street address**, **town** and **ZIP code**;
- its **neighbourhood**, which places it on the map and in distance search;
- **Show the street address publicly**: untick it for a private home or a shelter, and families
  see only the neighbourhood;
- **opening hours**, by day and season;
- **access**: wheelchair access, an elevator, an accessible restroom, a quiet space, finding the
  entrance, parking, and getting there by bus or train.

Until street-level locations are available, the map places a site at the centre of its
neighbourhood. **Close the site** keeps it on record but stops listings showing it; **Reopen the
site** brings it back.

**Staff.** **Invite someone** by email with a role and an optional job title. They get a link
(and choose a password first if they're new), then accept the invitation after signing in. Under
**Manage** you can change someone's role, choose which sites they work at, remove them, or
withdraw an invitation that hasn't been accepted.

Each person's **clearances** (background check, child abuse registry, fingerprinting, CPR and
first aid, safeguarding and mandated reporter training, and so on) show only their status and
expiry date. Reports from those checks are never kept on BrightTime.

## Listings

The **Listings** tab groups your listings by state: **drafts**, **waiting for review**, **changes
requested**, **published**, **unpublished**, **rejected** and **archived**.

**Writing a listing.** The editor has these sections:

| Section | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Basics | Title (short and plain: what it is, not who runs it), a one-sentence summary for search results, the description (plain text, a blank line between paragraphs), the kind (program, event, drop-in resource, or job or internship) and the language it's written in |
| Audience | Youngest and oldest age; lowest and highest grade (-1 for pre-K, 0 for kindergarten) |
| When | One date or every week (or every few weeks); first and last date; start and end time; days of the week; days with no session, such as holidays |
| Where | In person, online or both; which of your sites; the link to join online |
| Cost | Free, a fixed price, a sliding scale (lowest and highest price, and how it works), it varies, or participants are paid (jobs) |
| Registration | Through BrightTime, on your own website (with the link), or no registration (drop in); whether families can drop in without booking; places available; a deadline |
| Topics | The categories, interests and skills families filter by |
| Access and support | Meals provided, transport provided and getting there, languages spoken, anything else about access |
| Contact for families | An email and phone number |
| When it shows | **Show from** a date (empty means as soon as it's approved) and **take down after** a date |

**Preview as families will see it** shows the finished page and the next sessions, before anyone
else sees it.

**Getting it published.**
1. **Save draft** as often as you like.
2. **Save and submit**. A network organiser reviews it and either publishes it, asks for changes
   (with a note you'll see on the listing) or rejects it.
3. To change a listing that's waiting for review, **withdraw it from review** first.
4. Your organisation must be approved before any listing can be published.

**Changing a published listing.** Your edits go live at once, and a network organiser looks at the
change afterwards. **Take it off the site** (unpublish) asks you why; the organiser sees your
reason.

**Copy for a new season** makes a new draft with the same details. Choose the first session of the
new season: everything else moves by the same number of days, and a weekly session moves to the
next matching day. Give the copy a season name such as *Summer 2027*, check it, and submit it.

## Importing listings

Owners and admins can bring in many listings at once from **Import**.

1. Choose the format: a **CSV** spreadsheet (download the **CSV template**, which lists every
   column with an example row) or **HSDS 3.0 JSON** (Open Referral).
2. Upload the file. BrightTime checks every row first and shows **what the import would do** for
   each: a **new draft**, an **update**, **left alone** (waiting for review or no longer editable),
   or **not imported** with the error.
3. **Apply the import.** Rows with errors are skipped. New listings are drafts you submit as
   usual. Updates to published listings go live at once, and an organiser looks at them again.

Importing the same file twice doesn't create duplicates: rows are matched by your own id for each
listing. The column-by-column reference is in [docs/import.md](../import.md).

## Registration: forms, eligibility and waivers

These apply to listings that register **through BrightTime**. Families apply in the app: they
see your rules first, answer your form, sign your waivers and choose what you may see of the young
person. The website's family pages come later.

**Forms** (owners and admins) are the questions families answer when they apply.
1. **Start a new form** with a name and its first question.
2. **Add a question**: the question in English, its kind (short text, long text, number, date,
   yes or no, one choice, several choices, or an agreement to tick), whether it's **required**,
   help text, limits, the choices, and the question in other languages.
3. Mark a question **sensitive** if its answer must never be used in reports, exports or ranking.
4. **Publish.** A published version never changes, and each application keeps the version it
   answered. To change it, **make the next version**, which starts as a draft.
5. **Use this form** for a listing. A listing asks for one form; choosing another replaces it.
   **Retire** stops any listing starting to use a version.

**Eligibility** (owners and admins) says who can apply to an activity. Families see these rules
before they apply.
- Rules are by **age** (taken on a date you choose, usually the first day), **school grade**, or
  **where they live** (chosen neighbourhoods).
- Each rule either **must be met**, which makes an applicant ineligible when it isn't, or is
  **advice only**, which is shown but changes nothing.
- **Try the rules** against a made-up applicant to see what they'd say. Nothing is saved.

**Waivers** (owners and admins) are statements families sign, such as a photo release.
- Write the text (bold, lists and links are allowed), then **publish** it. Families sign the
  exact text of a published version, so it never changes. **Make the next version** to change it.
- **Ask families to sign** it on a listing, as **required** or **optional**. An application can't
  be submitted until every required waiver is signed.
- A parent or guardian signs for anyone under 18.
- The page counts signatures without showing anyone's.

## Applications, places and the waitlist

**Applications** lists the activities taking applications; choose one to see its queue. Each row
shows the applicant (as the family shares them, or *Name not shared*), when they applied, whether
they're eligible, the status, and their waitlist place.

**Places.** An owner or admin sets an activity's **places**, and optionally its **waitlist
places**. Nobody can be accepted until an activity has places. The page shows how many are taken
and how many are waiting.

**Deciding.** Open an application to see what the family shares, each eligibility rule (met, not
met, or can't tell yet), and the answers (sensitive ones are marked). Then **accept**, **put on
the waitlist**, **decline**, or **mark as being reviewed**, with an optional note. To decide many
at once, tick them in the queue and **apply to selected**. The page tells you which it left as
they were, and why. The last place always goes to exactly one applicant, even if two people
accept at the same moment.

**The waitlist.** When places free up, staff **offer places** to the front of the waitlist. Each family
offered a place takes it or declines; an offer they don't answer expires.

From an application you can also **Message the family**.

## Fees and payments

Owners and admins manage these under **Fees and payments**.

**Fees.** For each activity, add one or more fees: a name, an amount, how often (once, each
session, week, month or season), and which is the **default**. An activity with no fees is free.

**Price levels** lower a fee for households that fit them: by household size, by an income band
such as *under_35k*, or both, with the wording families see. Families pick the level they fit.
Nobody is asked their income, and none is kept.

**Payments.** Each activity's **Payments** page shows who owes what, how they're paying (**online**
or **in person**) and the status. You can:
- **Mark received**, with an optional note, for a payment made in person;
- **Waive** a fee, with a reason.

Card details never pass through BrightTime: online payments are made on the payment service's own
page. Where your county hasn't set up online payments, every payment is in person.

## Attendance

**In the console.** **Attendance** shows a week of your meetings; move between weeks with
**previous** and **next**. Open a meeting's **roster**:
- Mark each young person **present**, **late**, **left early**, **absent** or **excused**, then
  **save attendance** for the whole roster at once. You can mark from an hour before the meeting
  starts.
- To change a mark already saved, say **why**. The reason is kept with the change, and
  **corrected marks** lists every change.
- If some marks couldn't be saved, the reason is shown next to each name; the rest were saved.

**On a phone, without a signal.** In the app, open **Attendance**:
1. The first time, **Set up phone**: give the phone a name. It's then registered to send
   attendance for your organisation.
2. **Take roster** for one of today's meetings while you have a signal.
3. Mark it anywhere, signal or not. Marks are kept sealed on the phone.
4. **Send now** when you're back online. Each mark is sent once, however many times you press
   it. If someone else marked a person first, the app says so.

For safety, a phone that hasn't been online for 72 hours locks the roster until it reconnects.
If a phone is lost and its access withdrawn, it deletes everything it held the next time it
connects.

Families can also **check in** with a code on their phone that staff scan, or by scanning a code a
kiosk shows. The codes are built; the scanning and kiosk screens are still to come.

## Messages

**Messages** lists the conversations you're in with families.

- **Start one** from an application or a roster row (**Message the family**), with an optional
  subject.
- **No adult is ever alone in a conversation with a young person.** If a young person is in it,
  an admin of your organisation joins and reads along, and everyone in it is told so. If no admin
  can, BrightTime refuses to start it.
- A message an adult sends to a young person may be **held for a moderator** if it looks like an
  attempt to swap contact details. Only you see it, marked as held.
- **Announce to an activity's families** sends one message to every family with a child enrolled.
  Families don't reply and never see each other. A young person gets it only with consent and
  an admin reading along; the page tells you how many were left out.

## Reports

**Reports** makes a file when you download it, as **PDF**, **Excel** or **CSV**. Nothing is kept on
BrightTime afterwards.

| Report | What's in it |
|---|---|
| Roster | Everyone enrolled in an activity now, when they enrolled, and how many meetings they've attended |
| Attendance | An activity's attendance, each young person against each meeting, for a period |
| Participation | Counts across your activities, safe to share with a funder: small numbers are hidden, and the report says what it hid |

A name appears only where the family shares it with your organisation. Keep downloaded copies
safe: some name young people.

## Badges

Your organisation can award **digital badges** to the young people in your programs, in the Open
Badges standard, so they can show them anywhere that reads badges. The badges are built, and
their console screens are next. When they arrive:

- Owners and admins **define** a badge: its name, a description, the **criteria** (what someone
  did to earn it), and optionally how many months it lasts. It's a draft until it's
  **published**; after that it never changes. **Retire** stops it being awarded; badges already
  awarded stand.
- Staff **award** a published badge to a young person enrolled with you, with an optional note
  about what they did. Each young person holds a badge once.
- Owners and admins can **revoke** an award, saying why. Anyone checking the badge sees at once
  that it was revoked, and the reason.
- Your organisation is the badge's issuer, under its own digital identity, so a badge says who
  vouched for it.

## Playlists

A **playlist** is a set of steps young people work through at their own pace, with one of your
badges at the end. Playlists are built behind the scenes too, and their console screens are next.
When they arrive:

- Staff write a playlist as a draft: a title, a description, whether the steps go in order, an
  age range and estimated hours, the badge it awards, and optionally a badge someone must already
  hold to start it.
- Each step is one of: **attend a listing** (done once their attendance is recorded), **an activity
  elsewhere** (a link), **a reflection** they write, or **a check-in with staff**. Any step can be
  marked for review, and a check-in always is. Steps are required unless you say otherwise.
- **Publish** it, and from then on it never changes, because everyone's progress refers to its
  steps. **Archive** stops anyone new starting; those already on it carry on.
- Staff **review** submitted steps: approve one, or send it back with a note saying what to do
  again. You see each young person only by the display name they chose for their portfolio.
- When someone finishes every required step, they're given the playlist's badge, signed by your
  organisation.

**Projects and skills.** When the screens arrive:
- **Projects.** Young people can send you projects they made about one of your activities. Staff
  **approve** one, or **send it back** with a note saying what to change. Only approved work can
  appear on a young person's portfolio, and if they change it, it needs approving again. You see the
  project and the young person's portfolio display name, nothing more.
- **Skills.** Staff can **endorse a skill** for a young person enrolled with you, such as
  *Teamwork* or *Field research*, at a level from beginning to advanced. It appears on their
  portfolio as endorsed. The skills come from the county's list, which network organisers keep in
  Taxonomy.

**Gating a program on a badge.** An activity's eligibility can now require a badge, or a
completed playlist, as a rule that must be met. A young person who doesn't hold it can't submit an
application. For now these two rules are set through the API; the console's eligibility page shows
them but doesn't add them yet.

## What you can see about families, and why

- **Only what each family shares with you.** A family chooses which members and which details your
  organisation sees, for one program or all of them, and until a date. Where a family hasn't
  shared a name, you see *Name not shared*.
- **Only while the share lasts.** If a family withdraws it, those details disappear from your
  screens and reports at once.
- **Every look is recorded.** Opening an application, a roster or a report that names a young
  person is written to an audit log that can't be edited.
- **Sensitive answers** are never used in reports or exports.
- **No home addresses** for young people, anywhere.
