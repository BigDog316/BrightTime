# BrightTime for young people

This guide is for you if you're 13 or over and have your own account. If you're younger, a parent
or guardian adds you to their account; their guide is [here](families.md).

## Getting your own account

There are two ways.

**Make one yourself.** In the app tap **Create an account**, or use the website's sign-in page.
1. Say the month and year you were born. We never ask for the day.
2. Give your email address. We send you a link.
3. Open the link, choose a password, and come back to sign in.

If you're under 13, BrightTime can't make you an account. Ask a parent or guardian to add you
from their own account, in the app or on the website.

**Take over the profile your parent made.** If a parent or guardian added you when you were
younger, they can give you your own sign-in once you're 13. You get an email with a link: open it,
choose a password, and sign in with that email address from then on. Your parent can still manage
your consents.

You can add a **passkey** under **Account → Passkeys**, so you sign in with your phone's
fingerprint, face or screen lock instead of a password.

## Finding things to do

Search works just as it does for everyone: by words, age, kind of activity (programs, events,
drop-in resources, jobs and internships), neighbourhood, price, and in the app **near me**. Jobs
and internships are listed too.

Signed in on the app, you also get:
- **Picked for you**: a feed that puts your **interests** first. Each card says why it's there.
- **Saved** activities that follow your account, with reminders a day before a deadline or an
  hour before a session.
- An **inbox** and notifications, with **quiet hours** when your phone stays silent.

## What your account can do

Under **Account**, the app lists **what your account can do**: whether you can sign up for
programs yourself, whether programs can message you, whether you can post in groups, and whether
you can upload photos of your work. It depends on your age and on consent from a parent or
guardian. A program can also confirm your account.

To sign up for programs yourself, you need to **agree to the terms of use**. Read them under
**Terms** in the app or on the website, and tap **I agree**.

## Messages

When a program writes to you, it shows under **Messages**, in **Account** or at the top of your
**Inbox**. Each conversation says who is in it. You can reply, except to an announcement. A
program can only message you once your consent allows it.

## Staying safe

- **You're signed out when you close the app, or after 30 minutes without using it.** That's so
  nobody else can use your account on a shared phone.
- **No adult is ever alone with you in a conversation.** If a program messages you, an admin of
  that organisation reads along, and you're told so.
- **BrightTime never keeps your home address or where you are.** *Use my location* picks the
  nearest neighbourhood once and forgets your position.
- **Sign out on all devices**, under **Account**, if you lose your phone or think someone else
  has used your account.

## Your portfolio and badges (app)

Find **Your portfolio** under **Account**.

**Your portfolio** is a record of what you've taken part in. The hours come from attendance:
each program you attend adds its sessions and hours.
- It's **private** until you choose to show it.
- Tap **Hide** on any record to leave it out of your total and anything you've shown. It stays
  in your own list, marked *Hidden*, and **Show again** puts it back. Nothing is deleted.
- **Change who sees it** lets you show it to **anyone with the link**, or make it **public**. To
  do that you choose a **link name** (like `kwesi-builds`) and a **display name**, which can't
  include your family name. The screen shows your link as you type it. Anyone you share the link
  with sees your display name, a headline and short bio you write, your hours and your records.
  Never your account, your legal name or which programs enrolled you.
- Once it's shown, **Share** sends the link, and **Open the page** shows you what others see.
- A link-only portfolio is never shown to search engines.
- Showing your portfolio isn't open to anyone under 13.

**Badges** are awarded by the organisations whose programs you take part in, for something you
did, like *River Surveyor*. They're listed under your records, with who gave each one and when.
- Each badge is a digital credential in the **Open Badges** standard, so you can take it to other
  places that read badges, such as a college application or a professional profile.
- The badge never names you. It carries a scrambled code that only proves it's yours when you show
  it.
- **Check it** opens the badge's page, where anyone can check it's real. **Share** sends that
  page to someone. If an organisation takes a badge back (for example, it was given by mistake),
  the page says so, with the reason, and your list marks it as **revoked**.

## Playlists, goals, skills and projects: coming soon

These are built behind the scenes; their screens are next. When they arrive:

**Playlists** are sets of steps an organisation puts together, like *Watershed Explorer*, with a
badge at the end.
- A step might be coming to one of the organisation's sessions, an activity somewhere else,
  writing a short reflection, or a check-in with a member of staff.
- Some playlists go in order, so a step opens once the ones before it are done. Some steps are
  checked by staff, who may ask you to do one again and say why.
- Finish every required step and the playlist is complete: you're given its badge.
- Some playlists, and some programs, are open only to people who hold a certain badge, or who have
  completed a certain playlist. The program's page says so before you apply.

**Goals, skills and projects.**
- **Goals** are your own aims, like *lead a river walk*. They're private: nobody else sees them.
- **Skills** come from your county's list. Add the ones you have and say how far along you are,
  from beginning to advanced. An organisation you're enrolled with can also **endorse** a skill,
  vouching that you showed it. You can remove any skill, endorsed or not.
- **Projects** are things you made, like a map or a write-up, about an activity you took part in.
  You send a project to that organisation, and its staff approve it or ask you to change something.
  Once it's approved you can show it on your portfolio. If you change it after that, it goes back
  to private until it's approved again.

**Taking it with you.** You'll be able to download your portfolio:
- as a **PDF** to print or attach, with your activities and your badges, each with the link where
  anyone can check it;
- your **badges** as Open Badges credentials, for a digital wallet or another platform;
- a **Comprehensive Learner Record**, a single credential holding your activities and badges that
  colleges and employers' systems can read. It's signed by your county's BrightTime, and each badge
  inside keeps its own organisation's signature.

Records you've hidden are left out, and your legal name never appears.
