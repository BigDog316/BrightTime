# BrightTime for network organisers

This guide is for the county's network organisers: the people who decide which organisations and
listings appear on BrightTime, and who run the site itself. Sign in, then use the links at the top
of the console: **Review queue**, **Taxonomy**, **Wording** and **Audit log**.

## The review queue

The **Review queue** has three lists, oldest first:

- **Organisations applying**: new applications and ones sent back after changes.
- **Listings waiting for review**: drafts that providers have submitted.
- **Published listings changed since review**: a provider's edit to a live listing goes live at
  once. Look these over, and unpublish anything that shouldn't be there.

## Reviewing an organisation

Open an organisation to see its kind, legal name, what it does, its website, its sites, its
active owners, and its history of decisions.

1. **Start review** marks it *under review*, so other organisers know someone has it.
2. Work through the **vetting checklist** and **save** it as you go:
   - **Contact verified**: you reached the owner on a phone number or address that the
     organisation's public record supports. Required to approve.
   - **Website and public details checked.**
   - **Safeguarding policy seen.**
   - **Insurance certificate expires**: the date on the certificate you saw. Some organisations
     need a current one to be approved; the checklist says when.
   - The checklist also shows whether the organisation **has an open site** (it needs one to be
     approved), how many **active owners** it has, and how many staff and mentors have **no
     current background check** recorded.
3. Decide:
   - **Approve**. Its submitted listings can then be published.
   - **Request changes**, saying what it should change or send. The organisation sees your note
     and sends the application back when it's ready.
   - **Reject**, saying why. The organisation sees your reason.

Later, you can **suspend** an approved organisation (saying why; it sees your reason) and
**reinstate** it.

## Reviewing a listing

A listing's review page shows it **as families will see it**. For a listing reviewed before, it
also shows **changes since the last review**, field by field: what it was then and what it is now.

Decide:
- **Approve and publish**. It appears in search within a minute. If the organisation isn't
  approved yet, you can't publish, but you can still send it back with a note.
- **Request changes**, saying what the provider should change.
- **Reject**, saying why. The provider will need to start a new listing.

For a published listing that changed since review:
- **The changes are fine** marks it reviewed.
- **Unpublish**, saying why it's coming down. The provider sees your reason. **Publish again**
  puts it back.

## Taxonomy

**Taxonomy** holds the words providers tag listings with and families filter by, in four
vocabularies: **categories**, **interests**, **accommodations** and **skills**.

For each term:
- a **code** of lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, like `after-school-meals`. It appears in
  links, filters and saved searches, so it can't change once made;
- its **names** in each language. English is required, and pages fall back to it when a language
  has no name;
- its **order** (lower numbers first), and for some vocabularies a parent **category**.

The **skills** vocabulary is what young people choose from for their portfolios, and what
organisations endorse. It starts empty: add the skills your county wants to recognise.

Saving a term updates the listings tagged with it in search shortly afterwards. **Retire** stops
editors and filters offering a term; listings already tagged keep it until they're re-tagged.
**Restore** brings it back.

## Wording

**Wording** lets you change any sentence on the site, in any of its languages, for your county
only.

1. **Find text or a key**: search for the words you see on the page.
2. Write **your wording** for the language you're in. Keep every part in curly brackets, such as
   `{organisation}`, exactly as it is: those parts are filled in when the page is shown.
3. **Save wording**. It shows at once.

**Wording you have changed** lists every override in the current language. **Go back to the
standard wording** removes one. Every change is in the audit log.

## The audit log

The **Audit log** lists what people did on your county's site, such as sign-ins, account changes
and every decision about organisations and listings: who, what, when, the outcome (allowed,
refused or error) and the detail. Newest first. Nothing in it can be edited or removed.

Filter it by **action** (the start of the action's name, such as `enrollment.`), by **person**
(their email address), by **subject id**, by **outcome**, and by a **from** and **to** date.

## Consent by signed form

Some consents a parent gives for a child under 13 need a signed form rather than an email,
because they disclose more about the child. When a family brings you a signed form, you confirm it
on BrightTime, and the consent is recorded as given on that evidence. The step is built behind
the scenes; its screen in the console is still to come.

## What you can't see

Being a network organiser doesn't give you young people's records. You see organisations,
listings and the audit log. A family's details are seen only by the organisations the family
shares them with. Platform administrators, who run the servers, can't see them either.
